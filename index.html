Why they differ

Japanese shoppers tend to take in a whole page and check the details before they trust a company, so Japanese sites put more on the screen: dates, notices, rankings and many options at once. One survey found that 56% of Japanese customers would switch businesses after a single bad experience.

American sites assume people skim. They show one message at a time, with a big photo, a short line and one button, and leave the details for later pages.

Color plays a part too. Red reads as bold and positive in Japan, while in the US it more often signals a warning.
